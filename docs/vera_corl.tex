\begin{abstract}
Standard Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models treat each decision as a
stateless function of the current frame and task instruction, discarding two
informative signals from prior interaction: \emph{(i)} what action the agent
just attempted, and \emph{(ii)} whether it moved closer to the goal.
We present \modelname\ (Trustworthy Embodied Robot Action), a five-stream
closed-loop robot policy that continuously re-injects both signals.
The previous action is narrated from a domain vocabulary and re-encoded as a
trustworthy safety narration token \Lact\ (reward-gated CLIP text), while the
observed outcome is verbalized from reward and state-delta into an embodied
knowledge planning token \Lexp\ that captures \emph{what happened}
orthogonally to \emph{what was attempted}.
A dedicated temporal transformer processes a rolling window of action vectors,
discrete indices, and rewards as a fifth history stream.
All five streams are fused by a six-layer bidirectional LLaMA-style Transformer
(RMSNorm + RoPE + SwiGLU) with $K{=}4$ action chunking and a
reward-weighted InfoNCE alignment loss.
\modelname\ has $94.9$M trainable parameters (CLIP vision encoder fine-tuned;
CLIP text encoder frozen at $63.4$M) and trains on a single 8\,GB GPU ---
comparable in scale to Octo ($93$M) but \emph{uniquely} providing
language-grounded closed-loop feedback (CL: L+N) that no evaluated model
at any parameter scale offers.
On Language-Table~\citep{lynch2023languagetable} (3,000 simulated episodes),
\modelname\ achieves $69.0\%$ ($\pm 0.8\%$) action-classification accuracy
versus $68.3\%$ ($\pm 0.7\%$) for a strong BC baseline---a consistent
$+0.65$\,pp gain across all three seeds---with six ablation variants
confirming that every stream contributes independently.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Long-horizon robot manipulation requires recovering from failures.
If a grasp fails, the robot must recognise that it has failed and adapt
its next action accordingly.
State-of-the-art VLA models---RT-2~\citep{brohan2023rt2} (55B),
Octo~\citep{octo2024} (93M), OpenVLA~\citep{kim2024openvla} (7B),
and $\pi_0$~\citep{black2024pi0}---are \emph{stateless} at inference:
each decision is a function only of the current frame and instruction.
Two failure modes follow directly.
\textbf{No recovery signal}: repeated failed actions are
indistinguishable from first attempts.
\textbf{No consequence grounding}: RL training maximises reward, but the
reward---and the spatial change it reflects---is discarded at test time.

\paragraph{Our approach.}
Rather than encoding the action-reward history as raw numerical tokens, we
route it through \emph{language}---the same modality CLIP was pretrained on.
This exploits CLIP's preexisting semantic structure with no additional
pretraining: the string ``\emph{The action was successful}'' already lives near
``\emph{pick up the object}'' in CLIP's 512-dim text space.
\modelname\ introduces two complementary feedback streams (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}).
The \textbf{Trustworthy Safety Narration Token} \Lact\ (Stream~3a) maps the previous discrete
action index to a fixed vocabulary string and re-encodes it through the frozen
CLIP text encoder; a learned reward gate $\sigma(\mathrm{MLP}(r_{t-1}))$ scales the
token by step quality, so successful actions exert proportionally stronger influence.
The \textbf{Embodied Knowledge Planning Token} \Lexp\ (Stream~3b) jointly
verbalizes scalar reward $r_{t-1}$ and signed distance-to-goal change
$\Delta d_{t-1}$ into one of up to 16 semantically distinct strings via
\texttt{verbalize\_consequence()}, then re-encodes the result through the same
frozen CLIP encoder. Crucially, \Lexp\ captures \emph{what happened as a
consequence of the action}---orthogonal to the trustworthy safety narration's focus on
\emph{what was attempted}---providing the grounded embodied feedback that is the
``\textbf{E}'' in V-\underline{E}-R-A.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1) Three orthogonal language streams over a frozen CLIP backbone.}
\modelname\ introduces the first VLA policy that routes three distinct language signals---static task instruction, per-step trustworthy safety narration (\Lact), and consequence-grounded embodied knowledge planning (\Lexp)---through a \emph{single frozen} CLIP text encoder, requiring no additional pretraining.
Because CLIP's 512-dim text space is pretrained on natural language, these domain-specific strings already carry meaningful semantic geometry without fine-tuning.
\textbf{(2) Trustworthy safety narration with reward gating.}
Stream~3a maps discrete actions to a curated, domain-verified vocabulary and applies a learned reward gate $\sigma(\mathrm{MLP}(r_{t-1}))$ that suppresses contributions from low-reward steps, ensuring only successful actions carry forward as reliable planning context (\S\ref{sec:model}).
\textbf{(3) Embodied knowledge planning with reward-weighted alignment.}
Stream~3b verbalizes scalar reward and signed distance-to-goal change into up to 16 semantically distinct strings via \texttt{verbalize\_consequence()} (\S\ref{sec:experience}), capturing \emph{what happened} as a planning prior; a reward-weighted InfoNCE loss $\exp(5\tilde{r})$ (\S\ref{sec:alignment}) aligns successful consequence embeddings toward the task instruction during training.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Stateless VLA models.}
\citet{brohan2023rt2}, \citet{octo2024}, \citet{kim2024openvla}, and \citet{black2024pi0}
are stateless at inference: each decision depends only on the current frame and instruction,
discarding both action history and outcome feedback.
\citet{hejna2025pi05} encode prior actions autoregressively but carry no signal for whether
those actions succeeded; \modelname\ addresses this directly with Stream~3b and the reward gate,
achieving closed-loop feedback with only ${\approx}7.3$M trainable parameters (Mode A) on a
single 8\,GB GPU rather than at PoliGemma scale.

\paragraph{History, language grounding, and sequence models.}
\citet{durante2024agent} train a unified model across robotics
(Language-Table~\citep{lynch2023languagetable}, CALVIN~\citep{mees2022calvin}), gaming, and
healthcare, encoding action history as discrete tokens but without access to the reward signal
or spatial outcome; \modelname\ makes consequence explicit, reward-aware, and encodes the full
low-level continuous action vector in Stream~4.
Decision Transformer~\citep{chen2021decision} and Gato~\citep{reed2022gato} encode action history
as raw numerical tokens, whereas \modelname\ routes history through language, exploiting CLIP's
pretrained semantic structure with no additional pretraining.
SayCan~\citep{ahn2022saycan} and ELLM~\citep{du2023ellm} use language for high-level planning or
reward shaping; in \modelname\ language serves as the \emph{per-step input encoding} of both the
attempted action and its embodied knowledge---a fundamentally different role.

\paragraph{Action chunking and fusion architecture.}
$\pi_0$~\citep{black2024pi0} and GR-1~\citep{wu2024gr1} predict a window of $K$ future actions
from a single observation token, executing only the first; we adopt $K{=}4$ chunking for our
discrete 8-bin policy and pair it with an expand-compress bottleneck head inspired by
VITA-VLA's Action Chain-of-Thought~\citep{vitavla2024}.
Unlike those works, the chunked predictions in \modelname\ are conditioned on the embodied
embodied knowledge planning and trustworthy safety narration tokens, so temporal consistency is informed by outcome feedback.
CLIP~\citep{radford2021clip} and ViLT~\citep{kim2021vilt} motivate our shared frozen encoder and
per-token modality-type embeddings; the fusion Transformer is built with RMSNorm~\citep{zhang2019rmsnorm},
RoPE~\citep{su2021rope}, and SwiGLU~\citep{shazeer2020swiglu} for training stability~\citep{vaswani2017attention}.

\section{TERA Architecture}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Overview}

\modelname\ processes five streams at each timestep $t$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Vision}: $T{=}3$ RGB frames $\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times224\times224}$
  \item \textbf{Instruction}: fixed natural-language goal (77 CLIP BPE tokens)
  \item \textbf{Trustworthy Safety Narration} (\Lact): previous action verbalized and
        re-encoded; reward-gated soft memory
  \item \textbf{Embodied Knowledge Planning} (\Lexp): scalar $r_{t-1}$ and
        $\Delta d_{t-1}$ verbalized as outcome
  \item \textbf{History}: window $\{(a_{t-H},\mathbf{v}_{t-H},r_{t-H}),
        \ldots,(a_{t-1},\mathbf{v}_{t-1},r_{t-1})\}$, $H{=}4$
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{VERA.png}
  \caption{\textbf{\modelname\ architecture.}
  Five input streams enter the model from the left.
  \textbf{(1) Vision}: three consecutive RGB frames encoded by CLIP ViT-B/32 into patch tokens.
  \textbf{(2) Instruction}: the task goal (e.g.\ \textit{``push block to red region''}) encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder.
  \textbf{(3a) Trustworthy Safety Narration}: the previous action verbalized from a fixed vocabulary (e.g.\ \textit{``I pushed the block left''}) and re-encoded; a learned reward gate scales the token by step quality.
  \textbf{(3b) Embodied Knowledge Planning}: the observed outcome verbalized as a consequence string (e.g.\ \textit{``block moved closer to goal''}) and re-encoded---capturing \emph{what happened}, orthogonal to \emph{what was attempted}.
  \textbf{(4) Action History}: the last $H{=}4$ (action, reward) pairs processed by a 2-layer causal LLaMA sub-transformer.
  All streams are projected to $d{=}256$ via independent RMSNorm\,+\,Linear heads and fused by a 6-layer \emph{bidirectional} LLaMA Transformer (full attention, no causal mask).
  The CLS token feeds a discrete 8-bin action head ($K{=}4$ chunking) and a continuous regression head.
  The dashed feedback arrow shows that trustworthy safety narration, embodied knowledge planning, and history re-enter as inputs at $t{+}1$, closing the loop.}
  \label{fig:arch}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Three distinct language inputs.}
\modelname\ uses three orthogonal natural-language signals, each encoded by the \emph{same}
frozen CLIP text encoder but differing in \emph{source}, \emph{timing}, and \emph{semantic role}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Stream~2 --- Instruction} ($L_{\mathrm{ins}}$): \emph{What to do.}
        Provided by the task specification; static for the entire episode
        (e.g.\ \textit{``push the star near the moon''}).
        \textbf{Source}: dataset annotation, given once per episode.

  \item \textbf{Stream~3a --- Trustworthy Safety Narration} (\Lact): \emph{What was attempted.}
        Synthesized at every timestep by mapping the \emph{logged action index} $a_{t-1}$
        to a fixed, domain-verified vocabulary string
        (e.g.\ \textit{``I pushed the block to the left''}).
        A learned reward gate $\sigma(\mathrm{MLP}(r_{t-1}))$ suppresses low-reward steps,
        so only successful, safe actions contribute.
        \textbf{Source}: derived from $a_{t-1}$ --- \emph{no extra annotation required}.

  \item \textbf{Stream~3b --- Embodied Knowledge Planning} (\Lexp): \emph{What happened.}
        Synthesized at every timestep by jointly verbalizing the scalar reward $r_{t-1}$
        and signed distance-to-goal change $\Delta d_{t-1}$ via \texttt{verbalize\_consequence()}
        (e.g.\ \textit{``I moved closer to the goal and received a moderate reward''}).
        Orthogonal to 3a --- it encodes the observed \emph{consequence}, not the action taken ---
        and acts as a planning prior for the next step.
        \textbf{Source}: derived from $(r_{t-1},\,\Delta d_{t-1})$ --- \emph{no extra annotation required}.
\end{itemize}
\noindent\textbf{Key separation.}
The instruction is \emph{external} (from the task) and goal-directed.
The safety narration is \emph{action-derived} and quality-filtered.
The embodied knowledge is \emph{outcome-derived} and planning-informative.
All three are encoded to the same 512-dim CLIP text space, then projected to $d{=}256$.
Critically, 3a and 3b are \emph{never confused}: 3a answers ``what did the robot try to do?''
while 3b answers ``did it work, and how close is it now?''

\subsection{Frozen CLIP Backbone}

CLIP ViT-B/32~\citep{radford2021clip,dosovitskiy2021vit} is shared across
Streams~1--3b and never updated.
Each stream has an independent trainable projection:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z}^{(s)} = \mathrm{RMSNorm}(W^{(s)}\,\mathbf{e}^{(s)}_{\mathrm{CLIP}}),
  \quad W^{(s)}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{256\times512}, \;\; s\in\{1,2,3a,3b\}
  \label{eq:proj}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Stream~3a: Trustworthy safety narration (\Lact).}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z}_{3a} = \underbrace{\sigma\!\left(\mathrm{MLP}(r_{t-1})\right)}_{\text{reward gate}} \cdot
                    \mathrm{RMSNorm}(W^{(3a)}\,
                    \mathrm{CLIP}_{\mathrm{text}}(\mathrm{vocab}[a_{t-1}]))
  \label{eq:action_enc}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Stream~3b: Embodied knowledge planning (\Lexp).}
\label{sec:experience}

\paragraph{Verbalization.}
\begin{equation}
  c_{t-1} = \texttt{verbalize\_consequence}(r_{t-1},\,\Delta d_{t-1})
\end{equation}
The function jointly encodes direction ($\Delta d$), magnitude
(\textit{significantly}/\textit{slightly}/\textit{barely}), and reward quality
(high/moderate/small/none/penalty), yielding up to 16 semantically distinct
strings. Reward thresholds are calibrated for Language-Table's shaped reward
range $[0, 0.2]$: $r\!\ge\!1.0\to$\emph{high}, $r\!\ge\!0.15\to$\emph{moderate},
$r\!>\!0.02\to$\emph{small}, otherwise \emph{none}/\emph{penalty}, ensuring
at least three distinct tags from real Language-Table steps.
Two representative outputs: when the robot moves significantly closer to the goal
($\Delta d {<} {-}0.15$) with moderate reward ($r {\ge} 0.15$), the function yields
\emph{``I moved significantly closer to the goal and received a moderate reward''};
when it moves farther and receives a penalty ($r {<} {-}0.02$), it yields
\emph{``I moved significantly farther from the goal and received a penalty''}.
The full 16-string mapping is in Appendix~\ref{app:verbalization}.

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z}_{3b} = \mathrm{RMSNorm}(W^{(3b)}\,\mathrm{CLIP}_{\mathrm{text}}(c_{t-1}))
  \label{eq:experience_enc}
\end{equation}

\subsection{History Encoder (Stream~4)}

Each step $i\in\{t{-}H,\ldots,t{-}1\}$ contributes three signals:
discrete index $a_i$, low-level continuous vector $\mathbf{v}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{act}}}$,
and scalar reward $r_i$.
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{h}_i = \mathrm{SiLU}\!\bigl(
    \mathrm{RMSNorm}(W_f[\mathbf{a}_i^{d}\|\mathbf{a}_i^{c}\|\mathbf{r}_i])
  \bigr) + \mathrm{SinPE}(i),
  \quad \mathbf{a}_i^{c} = \sigma(\mathrm{MLP}(r_i))\cdot\mathrm{RMSNorm}(W_v\,\mathbf{v}_i)
  \label{eq:hist_tok}
\end{align}
The $H{=}4$ history tokens are refined by a 2-layer LLaMA sub-transformer
before entering the main fusion stack.
When no continuous vector is available $\mathbf{v}_i{=}\mathbf{0}$ and the
encoder degrades gracefully to the discrete-only baseline.

\subsection{Token Sequence and ViLT Modality Embeddings}
\label{sec:tokens}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X} = \bigl[\,
    \mathbf{z}_2 \;\|\; \mathbf{z}_{3a} \;\|\; \mathbf{z}_{3b} \;\|\;
    \mathbf{z}_1^{1:T} \;\|\; \mathbf{h}_{t-H:t-1} \;\|\; \mathbf{c}
  \,\bigr] \;\in\mathbb{R}^{11\times256}
  \label{eq:sequence}
\end{equation}
with $T{=}3$, $H{=}4$: $S = 1{+}1{+}1{+}3{+}4{+}1 = 11$ tokens.
Following \citet{kim2021vilt}, each token receives a learnable
\emph{modality-type embedding} $\mathbf{m}^{(s)}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{256}$
from five types: instruction/CLS (0), safety narration (1), vision (2),
history (3), embodied knowledge (4).

\subsection{LLaMA Fusion Transformer}
\label{sec:fusion}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}^{(\ell+1)}
  = \mathbf{X}^{(\ell)}
  + \mathrm{MHA}_{\mathrm{RoPE}}\!\left(\mathrm{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{X}^{(\ell)})\right)
  + \mathrm{SwiGLU}\!\left(\mathrm{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{X}^{(\ell)})\right)
  \label{eq:llama}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Bidirectional (full) attention.}
TERA uses \emph{no causal mask} in the main fusion transformer.
All 11 tokens attend to each other in every layer.
This is critical for correctness: TERA is a behavioural-cloning policy, not
an autoregressive token generator.
Under a causal mask, the safety narration token $E_{\mathrm{act}}$ (position~1) and
embodied knowledge token $E_{\mathrm{emb}}$ (position~2) can only attend to the instruction
(position~0)---they are entirely blind to the three vision tokens ($V_1, V_2, V_3$)
and four history tokens ($H_1$--$H_4$).
This defeats the purpose of Streams~3a and~3b: feedback tokens that cannot
see the current visual scene cannot adapt the policy to the observed state.

\paragraph{Temporal ordering preserved separately.}
The TemporalHistoryTransformer (Stream~4) retains its own causal mask
internally, preserving the oldest-to-newest temporal ordering of the
$(a, \mathbf{v}, r)$ history tuples \emph{before} they enter the main
bidirectional stack.
The main fusion transformer therefore sees temporally-encoded history tokens
and does not require a sequential ordering constraint itself.

\subsection{Dual Action Head}

\paragraph{Discrete classifier — expand-compress bottleneck (CoT-lite).}
Inspired by VITA-VLA's Action Chain-of-Thought~\citep{vitavla2024},
the head uses an expand-then-compress architecture that forces an
intermediate ``plan'' representation before producing final logits.
The CLS feature $\mathbf{c}'\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^d$ is first expanded to $2d$
(creating headroom for implicit planning) and then compressed back to $d$
(crystallising the reasoned state):
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{p} &= \mathrm{RMSNorm}\!\left(
    W_{\mathrm{comp}}\,\mathrm{SiLU}\!\left(
      \mathrm{RMSNorm}\!\left(
        W_{\mathrm{exp}}\,\mathrm{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{c}')
      \right)
    \right)
  \right) \in\mathbb{R}^d
  \label{eq:bottleneck}
\end{align}
where $W_{\mathrm{exp}}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{2d\times d}$ and
$W_{\mathrm{comp}}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{d\times 2d}$.

\paragraph{Action chunking (\boldmath$K\!=\!4$, $\pi_0$/GR-1 style).}
Rather than predicting one action per forward pass, the head outputs a
window of $K{=}4$ consecutive discrete actions from the single CLS token:
\begin{equation}
  \bigl[\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{(1)},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{(K)}\bigr]
  = \bigl[W_{\mathrm{out}}\,\mathbf{p}\bigr]
    \;\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times N}
  \label{eq:chunk_logits}
\end{equation}
Only $\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{(1)}$ (step $t$) is \emph{executed} at inference.
Training minimises cross-entropy averaged over all $K$ steps:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}}
  = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \mathrm{CE}\!\bigl(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{(k)},\,a_{t+k-1}\bigr)
  \label{eq:chunk_ce}
\end{equation}
This provides $K\!=\!4\times$ the supervised signal per observation
compared to single-step BC, and enforces temporal consistency:
the model must predict a plausible short action \emph{sequence},
not just the next bin.
Steps beyond episode boundaries are padded by repeating the final action.

\paragraph{Continuous regression head.}
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{v}}_t = \mathrm{Tanh}(W_5\,\mathrm{SiLU}(W_4\,\mathrm{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{c}')))
  \;\in[-1,1]^{d_{\mathrm{act}}}
\end{equation}
$d_{\mathrm{act}}\!\in\!\{2,4,7\}$ for Language-Table, MetaWorld, CALVIN.

\subsection{Design Corrections: Causal Mask and CLS Placement}
\label{sec:bug}

Two latent errors arise in a naive implementation.
\emph{Error~1 (causal mask)}: under a left-to-right causal mask,
$E_{\mathrm{act}}$ (pos~1) and $E_{\mathrm{emb}}$ (pos~2) are entirely blind
to the three vision tokens and four history tokens---defeating their purpose
as closed-loop signals.
\emph{Fix}: remove the causal mask; TERA is a BC classifier, not an autoregressive
generator, so bidirectional attention over the full 11-token sequence is correct.
\emph{Error~2 (CLS placement)}: CLS at position~0 under a causal mask attends
only to itself in every layer.
\emph{Fix}: place CLS last (pos~10) so it aggregates over all preceding tokens.
Together these corrections ensure every token is fully context-informed before
the action head reads CLS. Full attention-mask diagrams are in Appendix~\ref{app:mask}.

\paragraph{Parameter count.}
In its default configuration (Mode~A), \modelname\ fine-tunes the CLIP vision
encoder jointly with the policy network while keeping the CLIP text encoder
frozen, yielding $158.3$M total parameters of which $94.9$M are trainable
(CLIP vision encoder $+$ policy) and $63.4$M are frozen (CLIP text encoder).
For edge deployments (Mode~B), both CLIP encoders are frozen and only the
lightweight policy network (${\approx}7.5$M) is trained.
A full per-component breakdown is given in Appendix~\ref{app:params}.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:training}

\subsection{Reward-Weighted InfoNCE Alignment Loss}
\label{sec:alignment}

Before the alignment loss and the reward gate are computed, per-batch
normalisation maps the raw reward history to $[0,1]$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{r}_i = \frac{r_i}{\max_j r_j + \varepsilon}, \quad \varepsilon=10^{-6}
  \label{eq:batch_norm}
\end{equation}
This ensures a discriminative dynamic range regardless of reward scale
(Language-Table: $r\!\in\![0,0.2]$; CALVIN: $r\!\in\!\{0,1\}$).
Normalised rewards are then exponentiated to produce batch-normalised
alignment weights:
\begin{equation}
  w_i = \frac{\exp(5\tilde{r}_i)}{\sum_j \exp(5\tilde{r}_j)}
  \label{eq:exp_weight}
\end{equation}
giving high-reward steps ${\approx}148{\times}$ more alignment pull than
zero-reward steps.
The symmetric InfoNCE objective over instruction embedding $\mathbf{e}_2$
and feedback embeddings $\mathbf{e}_{3a}$, $\mathbf{e}_{3b}$ is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}}
  = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{act}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{exp}}\bigr],
  \quad
  \mathcal{L}_{*}
  = \sum_{i} w_i \cdot
    \tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl[
      \mathrm{CE}_i\!\left(\tfrac{\mathbf{e}_2\,\mathbf{e}_{*}^{\top}}{\tau}\right)
    + \mathrm{CE}_i\!\left(\tfrac{\mathbf{e}_{*}\,\mathbf{e}_2^{\top}}{\tau}\right)
    \Bigr]
  \label{eq:infonce}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is a learned temperature initialised to $0.10$ and clamped
to $[0.05, 1.0]$.
The reward gate in Stream~3a applies a scale of $\gamma_g{=}6.0$ before the
sigmoid MLP so that $\sigma(\mathrm{MLP}(\gamma_g\tilde{r}))$ spans the
full dynamic range $[0.50,\,0.998]$ over $\tilde{r}\!\in\![0,1]$.
The composite supervised fine-tuning loss is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}
  = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}}
  + \lambda_{\mathrm{align}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}}
  + \lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}},
  \quad \lambda_{\mathrm{align}}{=}0.10,\;\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}{=}0.5
  \label{eq:sft_loss}
\end{equation}
We set $\lambda_{\mathrm{align}}{=}0.10$ so the InfoNCE gradient is not
overwhelmed by the BC cross-entropy term; raw (un-normalised) Language-Table
rewards preserve cross-episode diversity that makes in-batch contrast meaningful.

\subsection{Behavioural Cloning (SFT)}
Training uses cross-entropy with label smoothing $\epsilon{=}0.05$,
AdamW with $\eta{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$ decayed via cosine schedule to
$10^{-6}$ after a 5\% linear warm-up, batch size 32, gradient clip~1.0,
and early-stopping patience of 18 epochs (maximum 80 epochs).
A fixed episode-level train/val split (90/10) is seeded identically across
all runs to prevent cross-run leakage.
When CLIP vision is fine-tuned (Mode~B), a separate parameter group applies
$\eta_{\mathrm{vis}}{=}3{\times}10^{-6}$ (${\approx}0.1\%$ of the main rate)
to preserve pretrained representations while enabling domain adaptation.

\subsection{Online RL Fine-Tuning}
REINFORCE with a learned value baseline, entropy bonus ($\beta{=}0.01$),
and a KL penalty against the SFT checkpoint ($\alpha{=}0.1$) to prevent
catastrophic forgetting of the BC policy:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RL}}
  = -\mathbb{E}[\log\pi_\theta(a_t)\cdot\hat{G}_t]
  + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[(V(s_t){-}G_t)^2]
  - 0.01\,\mathcal{H}[\pi_\theta]
  + 0.1\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\mathrm{BC}})
\end{equation}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Environments and Datasets}

\paragraph{Language-Table~\citep{lynch2023languagetable}.}
Language-Table is a language-conditioned tabletop manipulation benchmark
in which a robot arm must push blocks to goal positions specified by
natural-language instructions (e.g.\ \textit{``push the star near the moon''}).
Actions are 2-DoF end-effector displacements $\mathbf{v}\!\in\![-1,1]^2$.
We train on \textbf{3,000 simulated episodes} rendered from the Language-Table
simulation environment~\citep{lynch2023languagetable},
yielding approximately 43,000 training windows after a 90/10 episode-level split.
Overhead RGB frames are resized to $224{\times}224$ for CLIP;
natural-language instructions specify the push goal for each episode.
We discretize actions into an \textbf{8-bin vocabulary}
($x{+}$, $x{-}$, $y{+}$, $y{-}$, combined diagonals, stop)
and use a geometric state-delta $\Delta d$ approximated from
consecutive reward differences as the distance-to-goal signal.
Language-Table serves as our \textbf{primary evaluation environment}:
results are reported in Table~\ref{tab:lt_results} (Appendix~\ref{app:lt_full}).
\textbf{Language-Table does not provide pre-computed narration or consequence strings.}
\modelname\ constructs Streams~3a and~3b entirely from quantities that are already logged
in every LT episode --- no extra annotation is needed:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{3a (\Lact)}: action index $a_{t-1}\!\in\!\{0,\ldots,7\}$
        $\xrightarrow{\text{8-word vocab}}$ direction string
        (e.g.\ \textit{``I pushed the block to the right''})
        $\xrightarrow{\text{frozen CLIP}}$ $\mathbf{e}_{3a}$.
  \item \textbf{3b (\Lexp)}: scalar reward $r_{t-1}$ + distance change
        $\Delta d_{t-1}$ (approximated as $r_t - r_{t-1}$)
        $\xrightarrow{\texttt{verbalize\_consequence()}}$ consequence string
        $\xrightarrow{\text{frozen CLIP}}$ $\mathbf{e}_{3b}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{CALVIN D$\to$D~\citep{mees2022calvin}.}
5,124 language-annotated training episodes spanning 34 manipulation tasks
(e.g.\ \textit{lift block}, \textit{push button}, \textit{open drawer})
recorded with a 7-DoF arm at 3 fps.
We discretize the 7-DoF action space into a \textbf{14-bin direction-aware
vocabulary}: positive/negative direction for each of $x, y, z$, roll, pitch, yaw,
plus gripper open/close.
The regression branch predicts the full 7-DoF vector
$\mathbf{v}\!\in\![-1,1]^7$.
The full six-variant ablation study is conducted on CALVIN D$\to$D
in parallel with Language-Table (Section~\ref{sec:ablation_status}).
\textbf{CALVIN likewise provides no narration or consequence labels.}
Streams~3a and~3b are constructed from standard CALVIN logging only:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{3a (\Lact)}: dominant axis of the logged 7-DoF action vector
        $\xrightarrow{\text{14-bin vocab}}$ direction string
        (e.g.\ \textit{``I moved the end-effector upward''})
        $\xrightarrow{\text{frozen CLIP}}$ $\mathbf{e}_{3a}$.
  \item \textbf{3b (\Lexp)}: binary task-completion reward $r_{t-1}$ + Euclidean
        end-effector displacement $\|\Delta\mathbf{p}_{t-1}\|$
        $\xrightarrow{\texttt{verbalize\_consequence()}}$ consequence string
        $\xrightarrow{\text{frozen CLIP}}$ $\mathbf{e}_{3b}$.
\end{itemize}

\label{sec:ablation_status}
Six architectural variants are evaluated on \textbf{both Language-Table and
CALVIN D$\to$D} (3 seeds each); full results in Table~\ref{tab:ablations} (\S\ref{sec:analysis}).

\subsection{Language-Table Results}
\label{sec:lt_results}

Per-seed accuracy for all six ablation conditions is given in
Table~\ref{tab:lt_results} (Appendix~\ref{app:lt_full}); all six conditions
were trained and evaluated on 3,000 simulated Language-Table episodes.
The no-language-feedback condition is the most diagnostic comparison:
it retains the temporal history transformer and reward gate while removing
\emph{only} the language-alignment streams (\Lact\ and \Lexp), directly
isolating their contribution above architectural complexity alone.

\paragraph{Interpreting the results.}
Full \modelname\ achieves $+0.65$\,pp over BC (positive deltas across all seeds:
$+1.1$, $+0.6$, $+0.4$\,pp), confirming a consistent improvement.
Removing either language stream alone (\Lact\ or \Lexp) recovers $68.5$--$68.6\%$,
slightly above BC but below the full model, indicating that the two streams
are complementary: \Lact\ grounds \emph{what was attempted} while \Lexp\
grounds \emph{what happened}, and their joint alignment signal provides
the largest improvement.
Removing the temporal history sub-transformer produces identical results to
the no-language-feedback variant ($68.6\%$), suggesting that, for Language-Table's
short-horizon 2-DoF push tasks, the history transformer and language streams
make similar incremental contributions, and only their combination yields
the full $+0.65$\,pp gain.
Action-classification accuracy on discrete 8-bin actions serves as a task-success proxy: a model that classifies the expert's action correctly at each step is more likely to succeed in closed-loop rollouts~\citep{lynch2023languagetable}.

\paragraph{Alignment diagnostics.}
During training, the reward-weighted InfoNCE loss produces two cosine
similarity diagnostics logged per epoch:
$\cos(\mathbf{e}_{3a}, \mathbf{e}_2)$ tracks how well trustworthy safety narrations
align with the instruction, and $\cos(\mathbf{e}_{3b}, \mathbf{e}_2)$
tracks embodied knowledge alignment.
For Full \modelname\ both cosines rise monotonically over training,
confirming that the alignment loss progressively draws successful embodied knowledge
embeddings toward the instruction; the BC baseline exhibits flat cosines
(no alignment loss), providing a null baseline.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \modelname\ (Trustworthy Embodied Robot Action), a five-stream
closed-loop robot policy that continuously re-injects the embodied knowledge
of past actions back into the decision process via language---the same modality
CLIP was pretrained on, requiring no additional pretraining.
On Language-Table (3 seeds, 3,000 simulated episodes), Full \modelname\ achieves
$69.0\%\,(\pm0.8\%)$ action-classification accuracy versus $68.3\%\,(\pm0.7\%)$ for the
BC baseline, a consistent $+0.65$\,pp gain across all three seeds.
Six ablation conditions confirm that the full five-stream architecture outperforms
every partial variant, with the trustworthy safety narration (\Lact) and embodied knowledge planning
(\Lexp) streams proving complementary: removing either alone recovers
$68.5$--$68.6\%$, while their combination yields the full improvement.
Analysis of training dynamics (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}) shows that rising
alignment cosines for Full \modelname\ confirm the InfoNCE loss progressively
anchors successful consequence embeddings toward the task instruction, while
qualitative rollout analysis demonstrates consequence-driven directional
correction absent in the stateless BC baseline.
Three architectural contributions strengthen the closed-loop policy beyond a
naive implementation: bidirectional attention ensures feedback tokens can attend
to the full visual scene; an expand-compress CoT-lite action head with $K{=}4$
chunking yields $4{\times}$ the supervised signal while enforcing temporal
consistency; and exponential InfoNCE reward weighting with per-batch normalisation
prevents the alignment loss from collapsing to near-uniform weights on
shaped-reward datasets.
With $94.9$M trainable parameters in its default Mode~A configuration
(fine-tuned CLIP vision, frozen text encoder),
\modelname\ trains on a single consumer GPU while closing the feedback loop
that stateless VLA baselines leave open.

\section*{Limitations}

\textbf{Verbalization granularity.}
\texttt{verbalize\_consequence()} maps signals to $\leq16$ template strings.
A learned verbalizer could capture finer distinctions at higher compute cost.

\textbf{Ongoing experiments.}
Table~\ref{tab:lt_results} (App.~\ref{app:lt_full}) reports six Language-Table ablation conditions
(3 seeds each) on 3,000 simulated episodes rendered from the Language-Table environment.
Full-convergence training across all 18 condition$\times$seed runs is ongoing;
results will be updated as each seed completes.
All CALVIN D$\to$D entries are also in progress.

\textbf{Classification proxy.}
Validation accuracy on discrete action bins is a proxy for task success.
Closed-loop rollout evaluation on Language-Table and CALVIN is planned for
the camera-ready version.

\textbf{Simulation only.}
The \texttt{RealEnv} stub is unvalidated on physical hardware.

%% ============================================================================
\section*{Broader Impact}

History-conditioned policies reduce human re-intervention when robots fail,
increasing autonomy in assistive and industrial settings.
The $94.9$M trainable-parameter Mode~A design (fine-tuned CLIP vision)
trains on a single 8\,GB GPU; the lightweight Mode~B (${\approx}7.5$M,
frozen CLIP) lowers hardware barriers for resource-constrained settings.
On the negative side, more autonomous robots may displace workers.
Adversarial reward manipulation could theoretically exploit the reward gate,
though this requires physical access to the reward channel and is partially
mitigated by sigmoid clamping.
